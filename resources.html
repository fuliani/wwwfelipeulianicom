Frameworks

Ways of Thinking

Mental models I return to repeatedly when working through compensation and HR strategy problems.

⚙

The 3 P’s of Compensation

Position, Person, Performance. Every pay decision involves a role’s market value, the individual’s placement within range, and their performance contribution. Keeping these three dimensions visible prevents most comp errors.

☷

Architecture Before Compensation

You cannot build a sustainable pay program on an unclear job structure. The order of operations matters: define the architecture first, then price it. Most organizations get this backwards.

⚖

The Equity Triangle

External equity (vs. market), internal equity (vs. peers), and individual equity (vs. contribution). All three matter. Optimizing for one at the expense of the others creates problems.

📅

Total Rewards = Pay + Experience

Total rewards is not just benefits plus salary. It includes the full work experience: flexibility, development, culture, and purpose. Organizations that only optimize the financial components miss half the picture.

Data & Tools

Useful Starting Points

Not vendor endorsements. These are commonly used tools in the compensation practitioner toolkit.

📊

Radford / Aon Surveys

The most widely used compensation surveys in tech, life sciences, and professional services. Good baseline for market pricing in these sectors.

📊

Mercer Total Remuneration Survey

Strongest for global and multi-country compensation benchmarking. Particularly useful in markets where other surveys have thin data.

📊

Willis Towers Watson Surveys

Strong for executive compensation, broad-based pay, and benefits benchmarking. The executive comp data is among the most comprehensive available.

📊

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (OES)

Free, government-sourced occupational wage data. Useful for public sector benchmarks and as a sanity check against commercial survey data.
