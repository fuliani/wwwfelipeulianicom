What Nobody Teaches New Leaders · Day 5 of 5

Culture Is What Your Team Does When You Leave the Room

Psychological safety is not a value on a wall. It shows up in who speaks in calibration and who asks for a raise. Here’s one small change that makes it visible.

Felipe Uliani · September 2026 · 3 min read
Articles › HR Leadership › What Nobody Teaches New Leaders

Psychological safety is not a value on a wall. It shows up in who speaks in the calibration meeting, who asks for a raise, and who never does.

Where This Shows Up

Most leaders judge their culture by what they see, and what they see is a team on its best behavior. The real signal is somewhere else. It is in who challenges a rating and who stays quiet, in who raises a pay question directly and who takes it to a recruiter instead, in whether bad news travels up in days or in quarters. Those patterns are rarely about individual courage. They are about what people have watched happen to the last person who spoke up.

The Practice

The practice is a small shift rather than a program — the kind of 2-degree change I first heard described in Onsite’s Emotionally Smart Leadership course, where a minor adjustment, held over time, changes where you end up. For the next month, change one thing in the meetings you run:

  • Speak last. Give your view only after everyone else has given theirs.
  • Ask the quietest person in the room a specific question, not “any thoughts?”
  • When someone disagrees with you, thank them before you respond to the content.

None of these requires a budget or an announcement. They change what the team observes, which is the only thing that changes what the team believes is safe.

The Proof

In my experience, the people who never ask about their pay are not the most satisfied ones. They are often the ones who have concluded that asking is risky. They do not complain. They leave, and the exit interview is the first honest conversation anyone has with them.

The Reframe

Culture is not what a leader says the team values. It is what the team does when the leader is not there to see it — and that is built in small, repeated moments, not in statements.

That closes the series. Five skills, none of them on the job description: leaving the old job behind, managing your own pressure, building honest feedback, being clear, and making it safe to speak. If one of them is the conversation your leadership team needs to have, I’m glad to talk.
