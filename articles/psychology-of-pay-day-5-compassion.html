The Compassion Trap Hiding in Off-Cycle Pay Exceptions

A one-time fix for a recurring problem isn’t compassion. It’s a bandage. Here’s the triage question that tells the two apart.

Felipe Uliani · July 2026 · 3 min read
Articles › Compensation Strategy › The Psychology of Pay

A one-time fix for a recurring problem isn’t compassion. It’s a compassionate-looking bandage that quietly lets a structural gap keep recurring.

Where This Shows Up

It shows up when a manager approves an off-cycle pay adjustment to solve someone’s immediate hardship, without stepping back to ask whether the same gap will resurface for someone else in the same pay band next quarter. The exception feels like the right call in the moment — and often is, for that one person. But if the root cause is structural rather than individual, the exception fixes nothing beyond the single case, and the underlying gap keeps generating new hardship cases on its own schedule.

The Practice

The practice is a hardship-vs-structural triage question, asked before any off-cycle adjustment is approved: is this an individual, one-off situation, or a structural gap in the pay band that will keep producing cases like it? Individual situations get the exception, approved and documented as such. Structural causes get routed to a compensation review instead of a one-time fix — because the exception, however well-intentioned, isn’t the tool that closes a structural gap.

The Proof

In my experience, the exceptions that never get escalated to a structural review are the ones that quietly compound. A one-off adjustment this quarter looks generous. Three more just like it over two years, never connected to each other, start to look like an unexamined equity gap.

The Reframe

Compassion in compensation isn’t the exception you’re willing to approve. It’s asking whether that exception should have been a policy change instead.
