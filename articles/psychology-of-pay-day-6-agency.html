The “Path to Next Band” Conversation Every Review Needs

Most employees don’t want a bigger number. They want to know what moves the number. Here’s the conversation that gives them agency over their own pay.

Felipe Uliani · July 2026 · 3 min read
Articles › HR Leadership › The Psychology of Pay

Most employees don’t want a bigger number. They want to know what moves the number — and most companies never actually tell them.

Where This Shows Up

It shows up in the gap between the leveling guide and the performance review. Pay progression criteria exist on paper, documented in a job architecture or leveling framework somewhere in HR’s files. But that document rarely gets translated into something an individual employee can act on in their own role, in their own words. The criteria exist. The connection to a specific person’s next move doesn’t.

The Practice

The practice is a “path to next band” conversation, built into every performance review as a standing agenda item. It names the specific, controllable criteria that move someone from their current level to the next — not vague themes like “show more leadership,” but the actual behaviors and outcomes the leveling guide describes, translated into that person’s actual work. The conversation ends with something written down: not a promise of promotion, but a concrete answer to “what would move me.”

The Proof

In my experience, employees who can name their own path to the next band show up to performance conversations with a completely different posture than employees who only know their current number. One group is negotiating from clarity. The other is guessing.

The Reframe

Agency in compensation isn’t a bigger paycheck. It’s knowing exactly what would earn you one.
