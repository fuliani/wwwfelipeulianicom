Nobody Tells You Your Stress Is Now Contagious

As an individual contributor, a bad week is yours. As a leader, it becomes the team’s climate. Here’s the habit that keeps your pressure from becoming everyone’s.

Felipe Uliani · September 2026 · 3 min read
Articles › HR Leadership › What Nobody Teaches New Leaders

As an individual contributor, a bad week is yours. As a leader, it becomes the team’s climate — usually before you notice you are having one.

Where This Shows Up

It rarely looks like shouting. It looks like shorter replies, a meeting moved twice, a question answered with another question. The team reads all of it, because reading the leader is part of how people stay safe at work. They start to time their requests, soften their updates and hold back the bad news until it is polished. The leader, who feels they are coping well, is now working with less information than anyone in the room. The pressure did not stay with one person. It was passed down, and it came back as silence.

The Practice

The practice is a pressure check before the moments that carry the most weight: one-on-ones, reviews, any conversation about pay or performance. It takes a minute, and it has three parts:

  • What am I carrying into this room that has nothing to do with the person in front of me?
  • If it is going to show anyway, can I name it in one sentence so they do not have to guess?
  • Where does this pressure actually get released — and is that place somewhere other than my team?

There is a difference between managing how stress looks and dealing with where it comes from. The first is a performance, and teams see through it quickly. The second is the real work, and it happens outside the meeting.

The Proof

In my experience, the hardest compensation and performance conversations go wrong less because of the message and more because of the state of the person delivering it. The same decision lands as fair or as careless depending on what the leader walked in carrying.

The Reframe

Self-management is not about looking calm. It is about making sure the team is reacting to the work, and not to your week.
