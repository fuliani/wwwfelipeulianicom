The Self-Awareness Check Before Any High-Stakes Pay Call

The best comp leaders don’t have the most frameworks. They know exactly which of their own instincts to distrust before a pay decision is final.

Felipe Uliani · July 2026 · 3 min read
Articles › HR Leadership › The Psychology of Pay

About this series: A few months ago I went through a leadership development program on emotional intelligence and inclusive leadership. This is Day 1 of a 7-part series translating what it surfaced into Total Rewards practice.

The best comp leaders I know aren’t the ones with the most frameworks — they’re the ones who know exactly which of their own instincts to distrust in a specific, high-stakes moment.

Where This Shows Up

That moment is usually this: defending a pay decision you’ve already made, rather than genuinely evaluating one that’s still open. It happens in market pricing calls, in exception approvals, in the final sign-off on an outlier merit increase. By the time a decision reaches that stage, most leaders aren’t asking “is this right” anymore — they’re asking “how do I justify what I already decided.” No framework catches that shift. It has to be caught by the person making the call, in the moment, before the decision hardens.

The Practice

The practice is a single question, asked out loud, before any high-stakes pay call: “What’s the one instinct I’m most likely to trust too much in this exact type of decision?” Not a general reflection — a specific one, tied to the decision in front of you right now. For some leaders it’s overweighting tenure. For others it’s trusting a manager’s gut read over the data. Naming the instinct before deciding does two things:

  • It slows the moment down just enough to notice if the instinct is doing the deciding
  • It creates a natural trigger point for asking someone else to weigh in

The Proof

In my experience, leaders who name their own bias trigger explicitly, before finalizing a call, are more likely to bring in a second reviewer on outlier decisions. That’s not a personality trait. It’s a habit built by asking the question every time, until it becomes part of how the decision gets made rather than an afterthought.

The Reframe

Self-awareness isn’t a soft skill you either have or don’t. It’s a pay-decision safeguard you can build into the process, one question at a time, or leave out and hope your instincts were right.
